# Seam Rules

Every seam must be intentional. The page should feel like one coordinated composition, but not every section needs a dramatic visual transition.

## Hero To Story

Desired transition: the one major navy, cream, and gold swoop of the page.

Allowed graphic behavior: broad curved motion, gold signal accent, gentle depth, and matched geometry that visibly continues across the seam.

Forbidden graphic behavior: hard cut, unrelated wallpaper, baked text/buttons, or repeating the same giant wave later in the page.

Desktop approach: export Hero and Story from the same `1440px` master canvas with the swoop crossing the exact seam coordinate.

Mobile approach: redraw the swoop for mobile so it has enough vertical room and does not crush the hero or story safe zones.

Separate mobile slice required: yes.

## Story To Trust

Desired transition: calm settling from Story into a coded proof bar.

Allowed graphic behavior: quiet field, faint linework, small gold signal accents, and enough contrast for coded trust content.

Forbidden graphic behavior: dark wavy trust band, baked stats, baked icons, fake proof cards, or heavy motion behind the trust bar.

Desktop approach: use a narrow support slice or quiet lower Story edge that hands off to the coded Trust bar.

Mobile approach: keep the trust area flatter and taller if coded trust items stack.

Separate mobile slice required: yes when the trust bar stacks or needs more vertical breathing room.

## Trust To Practitioners

Desired transition: coded Trust bar releases into a light practitioner section.

Allowed graphic behavior: subtle linework at edges, light gold or bronze accents, and open fields behind card zones.

Forbidden graphic behavior: fake cards, heavy wave repetition, or motifs that look like trust stats continuing into practitioner cards.

Desktop approach: keep the seam understated and structured.

Mobile approach: protect stacked cards and let graphics sit above, below, or at the outer edges.

Separate mobile slice required: yes if practitioner cards move to a single-column stack.

## Practitioners To Model

Desired transition: light, structured handoff into the Living Well Today Model.

Allowed graphic behavior: data rail hints, faint diagnostic linework, and low-contrast botanical/vitality accents.

Forbidden graphic behavior: duplicating the coded model rail, drawing fake steps, or washing out a light section.

Desktop approach: align subtle structure from practitioner edges into the model area.

Mobile approach: simplify linework and increase vertical spacing around stacked model elements.

Separate mobile slice required: yes if the model rail changes from horizontal to stacked.

## Model To Technology/Catalog

Desired transition: shift from structured model explanation into feature-card utility.

Allowed graphic behavior: data rail and diagnostic orbit motifs that guide attention toward Technology/Catalog without becoming card UI.

Forbidden graphic behavior: fake card borders, fake product tiles, giant repeated wave, or busy linework behind card text.

Desktop approach: outer background can coordinate the two-card area while internal card art remains separate.

Mobile approach: each card stack needs clean gutters and simpler background detail.

Separate mobile slice required: yes.

## Technology/Catalog To Final CTA

Desired transition: visual release from feature cards into a focused final action.

Allowed graphic behavior: soft fade in energy, corner accents, or low-detail signal linework that frames the CTA.

Forbidden graphic behavior: another Hero-scale wave, baked CTA text/buttons, or product-card motifs entering the CTA copy zone.

Desktop approach: keep the lower Technology/Catalog background quiet and let Final CTA own its center field.

Mobile approach: taller CTA slice with strong copy/button safe zones.

Separate mobile slice required: yes.

## Final CTA To Footer

Desired transition: calm handoff to the coded footer.

Allowed graphic behavior: subtle bottom edge, small gold signal trace, or quiet navy/cream field.

Forbidden graphic behavior: baked footer, fake links, dark band that conflicts with the real footer, or unfinished cut-off motifs.

Desktop approach: final CTA bottom bleed should resolve before the footer begins.

Mobile approach: keep the bottom edge simple because footer content stacks.

Separate mobile slice required: yes if mobile footer spacing or background color differs.
